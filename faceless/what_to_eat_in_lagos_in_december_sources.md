## 5. Suya: thin-sliced spiced beef on skewers, grilled; popular evening street food (on screen pin: "Try it at Suya Junction, Obalende")
- Wikipedia, Suya: "smoke-grilled spiced meat on skewer ... generally made with thin-sliced spiced beef ..."; "most popular as evening street food". https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suya
- Detty List, "What to Eat in Lagos": "Suya: thinly sliced beef rubbed with spicy yaji and grilled ... Essential after a concert"; "Suya spots: roadside grills that come alive at night." https://www.dettylist.com/nigeria/lagos-food-guide
- Suya Junction, Obalende: Wikipedia Obalende ("famous ... for its suya with a junction popularly called Suya junction"); Ofadaa listing https://ofadaa.com/lagos/restaurants/obalende-suya-junction

## 4. Small chops = puff-puff, samosas, spring rolls; served at parties/events
- Detty List: "Small chops: puff-puff, samosas, spring rolls and gizzard skewers. Every event starts here."
- MealCondo, "What Nigerians Cook at Christmas": "small chops (puff puff, samosas, spring rolls, peppered gizzard)" https://www.mealcondo.com/blog/nigerian-christmas-food-traditions
- African Food Network, "15 Popular Nigerian Christmas Dishes": small chops "to serve guests as they arrive ... meat pies and spring rolls to puff puffs" https://afrifoodnetwork.com/articles/holidays/christmas/nigerian-christmas-dishes/

## 3. Pepper soup: spicy goat or catfish broth
- Detty List: "Pepper soup: spicy goat or catfish broth."
- African Food Network: "Goat, chicken, catfish, turkey, snails or beef simmer in a hearty pepper-infused broth".

## 2. Chapman was invented by a Nigerian hotelier (Sam Alamutu) for his wife, who didn't drink alcohol (surprising claim, 4 sources)
- BusinessDay, "Who invented Chapman?" (context from his daughter Sola Alamutu): created by hotelier Samuel Alamutu because his wife "would turn down any offer of alcoholic drink"; first served to the public at Ikoyi Hotel, Lagos. https://businessday.ng/bd-weekender/article/who-invented-chapman/
- Zikoko, "Wait, Who Invented Chapman?": "invented by Mr Sam Alamutu, a renowned professional hotelier ... his wife who didn't drink alcohol." https://www.zikoko.com/chopist/wait-who-invented-chapman/
- Food & Wine: "Sam Alamutu ... hotelier ... crafted the Chapman for his wife in the 60s because she preferred soft drinks to alcohol." https://www.foodandwine.com/drinks/chapman-nigerian-non-alcoholic-cocktail
- PUNCH: the Alamutu-for-his-wife history "is more widely held to be true". https://punchdrink.com/articles/chapman-nigeria-nonalcoholic-cocktail/
- Note: Wikipedia (Chapman (drink)) repeats the older "Ikoyi Club bartender made it for a customer named Chapman" legend; BusinessDay, Zikoko and PUNCH describe that as the popular but less supported story. The script avoids the name-origin claim and only states the Alamutu/wife account; "mocktail invented in Nigeria" is supported by all of the above.

## 1. Party jollof: centrepiece of the Christmas table, often served with fried rice
- MealCondo: "No Nigerian Christmas table is complete without a big pot of smoky party jollof ... Often it's served alongside fried rice."
- Detty List, "Christmas in Nigeria": "jollof or fried rice with chicken (or goat) is the classic Christmas meal"; "Jollof rice and fried rice, often both." https://www.dettylist.com/nigeria/christmas-in-nigeria
- African Food Network: "No list of treasured Nigerian Christmas dishes would be complete without the crown jewel itself - jollof rice."
